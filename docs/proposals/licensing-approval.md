## Recommendation and precise obligations

Apache-2.0 permits no-charge and commercial use, modification, sublicensing and source/object redistribution under its conditions. It does not require publishing all private modifications. Section 3 grants only the contributor-controlled patent claims necessarily infringed by the contribution alone or with the work; specified patent litigation terminates that patent grant. It is not a blanket patent clearance.

Derivative distributions must include the license (4(a)), prominently identify changed files (4(b)), preserve relevant copyright/patent/trademark/attribution notices in distributed derivative source (4(c)), and reproduce applicable upstream NOTICE attributions in an allowed location if a NOTICE accompanies the work (4(d)). A distributed NOTICE, accompanying source/documentation, or an appropriate existing third-party-notice display can satisfy 4(d). NOTICE is informational and cannot add restrictions. Sections 7–8 disclaim warranties and limit liability subject to applicable law. Section 6 gives no general trademark or endorsement permission.

MIT also permits free/commercial use, modification and redistribution, with copyright and permission notices preserved in copies or substantial portions, and warranty/liability disclaimers. BSD-3-Clause adds explicit source/binary notice handling and non-endorsement. Neither includes Apache's express contributor patent grant. All three require authority over licensed material; none clears third-party data/models or resolves privacy. See [comparison](../license-options.md) and [matrix](../dependency-licensing-matrix.md).

Apache-2.0 is recommended for contributor patent clarity and standard notice-based original-project credit. Compatibility remains component-specific: preserve permissive upstream notices, assess strong copyleft combinations separately, and do not relicense model/vendor terms. Apache-2.0 is not generally compatible with GPLv2-only combined works; GPLv3/AGPLv3 combinations require their applicable obligations.

## Proposed README / About wording

Use only after owner approval and actual license application:

> NajdiLab is free and open-source software licensed under Apache-2.0. Original project: NajdiLab. Original source: https://github.com/abinadwan/NajdiLab. When redistributing NajdiLab or derivative works, preserve the copyright, license and attribution notices required by the applicable licenses, and identify modifications. Third-party components and separately supplied data or models retain their own terms.

Recommended downstream visible credit:

> Based on NajdiLab — https://github.com/abinadwan/NajdiLab. Modified by [downstream author]. No endorsement by the original project is implied.

Legally required notice preservation differs from recommended visible README/About credit. Apache-2.0 does **not** universally require a clickable homepage link in every derivative product. Including the original repository in an approved NOTICE preserves applicable attribution through section 4(d)'s allowed locations; it does not mandate a particular product screen. A mandatory advertising/homepage credit condition would require custom terms or another license assessment, could reduce compatibility and must not be described as standard Apache-2.0. No such term is proposed here.

## Proposed contributor guidance

Until a license is approved, contributors must not assume an inbound/outbound license policy or permission to republish. Do not add real conversations, private fixtures, weights or unclear third-party code. Identify origins and existing terms for borrowed material, retain notices and confirm authority to submit (including employer rights).

After Apache-2.0 adoption, intentionally submitted contributions normally follow section 5 unless explicitly stated otherwise or a separate agreement applies. An optional DCO/sign-off policy would document contributor certification; it is not an ownership transfer, automatic CLA or substitute for rights review. Do not impose a new DCO/CLA without a maintainer decision. Review earlier contributions separately; later license adoption does not retroactively establish authority.
